  and Emacs and Vim grew into full environments.

  [0m[1mEclipse by the Graphical Interface[0m

  Graphical interfaces, popularized by the Macintosh in 1984 and Windows in the
  1990s, pushed text interfaces out of mainstream computing. Terminals remained
  the realm of system administrators, programmers and enthusiasts. The terminal
  became something you opened inside a window rather than the machine itself. Yet
  it did not die. Remote servers, embedded systems and scripting all benefitted
  from text's low bandwidth, composability and scriptability. SSH, [0m[38;2;177;185;249mtmux [0m(2007) and
  GNU Screen turned the terminal into a persistent, multiplexed workspace that
  survived disconnections.

  [0m[1mThe Modern Revival[0m

  Since about 2010, terminal interfaces have enjoyed a renaissance. Several
  factors drove it. Developers spend their days with Git, containers and cloud
  tooling, which are overwhelmingly command-line driven. Terminal emulators gained
  GPU acceleration, true-color support, ligatures and image protocols, with
  projects such as iTerm2, Kitty, Alacritty and WezTerm. Tools like [0m[38;2;177;185;249mhtop[0m, [0m[38;2;177;185;249mtig[0m,
  [0m[38;2;177;185;249mlazygit[0m, [0m[38;2;177;185;249mk9s [0mand [0m[38;2;177;185;249mbtop [0mshowed that text interfaces could be as interactive and
  polished as GUIs while running over SSH.

  New frameworks reflect the trend: Python's Textual and Rich, Go's Bubble Tea,
  Rust's Ratatui and JavaScript's Ink bring modern ideas like declarative layouts,
  component models and CSS-like styling into the terminal. Most recently, AI
  coding assistants have chosen the terminal as their primary home, because it
  sits where developers already work.

  [0m[1mConclusion[0m

  Terminal user interfaces have moved from necessity to choice. They began as the
  only way to talk to a computer, evolved into sophisticated text-mode
  applications, were overshadowed by graphics, and are now valued for speed,
  portability and keyboard-driven efficiency. Their durability comes from a simple
  idea that appeared with the first teletype: text is a universal medium, and a
  well-designed conversation with a machine never goes out of style.
