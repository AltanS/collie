[0m[38;2;255;255;255m●[0m [0m[1m[3m[4mThe History of Terminal User Interfaces[0m

  The terminal user interface, or TUI, is often treated as a relic that survived
  into the graphical age. In fact it is one of computing's oldest and most durable
  interaction models. Its history runs from mechanical teleprinters to modern
  frameworks that draw rich dashboards inside a text window.

  [0m[1mThe teleprinter era[0m

  Before there were screens, there were typewriters. In the 1960s, interacting
  with a computer usually meant a Teletype Model 33, a mechanical device that
  printed characters on paper and sent keystrokes down a serial line. The
  interface was a pure conversation: you typed a line, the machine replied with a
  line. This "glass teletype" model, a scrolling stream of text, shaped Unix and
  its command-line tradition. It was not yet a [0m[3muser interface [0min the spatial
  sense. Nothing could be redrawn, since ink on paper cannot be erased.
