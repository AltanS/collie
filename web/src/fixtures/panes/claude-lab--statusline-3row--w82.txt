[0m[38;2;255;255;255m●[0m [0m[1m[3m[4mA Brief History of Terminal User Interfaces[0m

  The terminal user interface, or TUI, is often treated as a relic that graphical
  systems replaced. Its history shows something different. It is a lineage of
  ideas that never stopped evolving, and it keeps returning whenever speed,
  portability, or simplicity matters.

  [0m[1mThe Teletype Era[0m

  Interactive computing began with the teletypewriter. In the 1960s, machines like
  the ASR-33 Teletype let a person type a command and receive printed output on
  paper. Interaction was strictly linear: you typed a line, the machine answered,
  and the paper scrolled on. There was no way to revise what had already been
  printed. The line-oriented model shaped everything that followed, including the
  shell prompt, the newline-terminated command, and the idea of a program as a
  filter on a stream of text.
