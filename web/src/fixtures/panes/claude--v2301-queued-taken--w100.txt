  Tomas packed his bag on Saturday. He stood at the top of the tower one last time. He put his hand
  on the old brass lens. It was cold. 

  Below, a small boat came across the water. The girl rowed it herself. She climbed the steps, out
  of breath, and held out a paper bag. Inside was a loaf of warm bread.
  
  "My father says thank you," she said. "He says the machine could not have done it."

  Tomas smiled. The machine would do fine on most nights. It would never be wrong about the time or
  the weather. But it would never hear a call, and it would never choose to answer.

  "Tell your father the machine is a good lamp," he said. "But someone must still watch the sea."

  The girl looked at him. "Then I will learn," she said.

  Tomas laughed for the first time in weeks. He took her hand and showed her the first flash of the
  signal.
